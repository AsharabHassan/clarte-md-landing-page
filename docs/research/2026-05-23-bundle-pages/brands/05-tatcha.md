# Tatcha — Ritual / Set Pages

**URLs examined:**
- `https://tatcha.com/pages/skincare-starter-sets` — Starter Rituals landing page (4 rituals by skin concern)
- `https://www.tatcha.com/category/skincare-starter-sets/` — alternate Starter Ritual Sets grid
- `https://www.tatcha.com/product/starter-ritual-ultra-hydrating-plumping-set/SA00041T.html` — The Starter Ritual: Hydrating & Plumping ($74 / $102 value)
- `https://www.tatcha.com/product/starter-ritual-firming-brightening-set/SA00041T.html` — The Starter Ritual: Firming & Smoothing ($95 / $140 value)
- `https://tatcha.com/products/the-starter-ritual-pore-refining-and-clarifying-set` — The Starter Ritual: Pore-Refining & Clarifying ($75 / $103 value)
- `https://www.tatcha.com/products/the-dewy-skin-cream` — PDP "Suggested Ritual" cross-sell section

**What they call it:** Set / Starter Ritual / Value Set. "Ritual" is the brand-level word; "Starter" qualifies entry-point; "Value Set" qualifies the savings angle. The landing page calls them simply "Starter Rituals." On individual set pages they become "Starter Ritual: {Concern} Value Set."

---

## Hero composition

**The Starter Rituals landing page (`/pages/skincare-starter-sets`)** opens with an eyebrow line: *"Reveal Your Glow in Just 2 Weeks"* — a time-bound promise, not a brand claim. Below it: *"Experience a complete Tatcha ritual with our new mini sets curated for each skin type (up to 32% value)."* The word "value" is parenthetical and understated; the lead is the promise (2 weeks) and the entry point (mini sizes, skin-type matching). Sets are displayed in a **vertical stacked format** below the headline — one set per row, each with a hero image, tagline, skin-type qualifier, and "Shop Now" CTA.

**Individual set PDPs** (e.g., Hydrating & Plumping, Pore-Refining & Clarifying) follow the standard Tatcha PDP template: breadcrumb reads `"Gifts & Sets"`, headline is `"The Starter Ritual"` in serif, sub-headline is `"{Concern} Value Set"` in sans. No dedicated landing-page hero band. Set pages live structurally as PDPs, not as editorial landings.

There is no separate lifecycle-of-the-skin or "why a ritual" intro paragraph on the individual set pages — the entry point is immediately: skin-type qualifier → price → products → CTA.

---

## Composition display

Individual set products are shown in a **carousel/slider format** (10–11 slides per set): slides include the full-set boxed shot, then unboxed contents, then individual product close-ups, then texture/application shots. Numbered `"Go to item 1"` navigation replaces dots — editorial pacing, not promotional snap.

Beneath the carousel, a **"How to Use" sequential visual guide** renders as a 4-step numbered flow: Step 1 cleanser → Step 2 treatment → Step 3 serum → Step 4 moisturizer. This is the closest analog to a protocol-sequenced composition display in the segment.

Products are listed as a **stacked, named list** (not linked individually) under a "What's Included" section:
- Product name + mini size + fluid oz
- Individual PDP links are **not** present within the set page product list

On the landing page, each ritual has a **tagline that names its hero product**: *"Featuring The Dewy Serum, proven to maintain 2x hydration all day"* (Hydrating); *"Featuring The Texture Tonic, proven to show a reduction in the look of pores after 2 weeks"* (Clarifying); *"Featuring The Silk Serum, proven to be 2x more effective than retinol"* (Firming). The hero-product-forward tagline is an unusual pattern — each ritual's credibility is anchored to its most clinically verifiable product.

Savings are surfaced as two numbers: **sale price** (large, primary) and **(${value} value)** as a parenthetical sub-note. There is no strikethrough on the regular price, no explicit "save $X" pull-quote, and no "% off" badge. The value bracket is consistently placed near the price but not emphasized visually — the set price is the hero, the value claim is the quiet reassurance.

The landing page uses *"up to 32% value"* in the hero sub-copy — the only instance of a percentage-savings figure at page level. Individual set pages do not restate this percentage.

Individual product prices are not disclosed on any of the set pages or the landing page.

## CTA strategy

**Single "Add to Bag" for the full set** on each individual set PDP — the button appears twice on the page (above and below the fold). No per-product add buttons. No "Add All to Bag" pattern on set PDPs.

**Landing page:** each set has a `"Shop Now"` CTA button — routes to the individual set PDP, not directly to cart.

**The Dewy Skin Cream PDP "Suggested Ritual" section:** the cross-sell 4-up (Dewy Skin Cream + 3 companions) carries an **`"Add All to Bag"` button** at section level, plus individual `"Add to Bag"` buttons per product card. This is the only Tatcha surface with a multi-product single-click purchase CTA. It is used for the PDP cross-sell, not for the dedicated set pages.

No subscription or subscribe-and-save option visible on any set or ritual page.

## Evidence integration

**Landing page (`/pages/skincare-starter-sets`):** each ritual carries one anchored clinical claim in the tagline, keyed to the hero product:
- *"proven to maintain 2x hydration all day"* (Dewy Serum — bio-instrumentation)
- *"proven to show a reduction in the look of pores after 2 weeks"* (Texture Tonic — clinical)
- *"shown to improve visible redness in 2 weeks"* (Indigo Calming Cream — clinical)
- *"proven to be 2x more effective than retinol"* (Silk Serum — 41 panelists, 4-week expert grading)

Claims are **above** the "Shop Now" CTA on the landing page — each ritual's tagline doubles as its clinical credibility line. Asterisked footnotes at page bottom carry panelist counts (ranging 25–40 per study).

**Individual set PDPs:** clinical claims appear within the product description for included items but are **not surfaced in a dedicated stats block**. The clinical claim is baked into the body-copy benefit line, not isolated as a number-forward visual element.

**The Dewy Skin Cream PDP (for "Suggested Ritual" reference):** clinical stats appear above-fold in the buy box:
- *"100% showed immediate improvement in skin plumpness, suppleness, and radiance"* (36 panelists, clinical study)
- *"100% showed all-day improvement in skin barrier after 1 use"* (30 panelists, bio-instrumentation)
- *"88% showed improvement in dry fine lines"* (2 weeks, 36 panelists)
- *"86% agreed the cream works well with makeup"* (1 week, 36 panelists)

The methodology footnote on the landing page cites *"clinical studies"* and *"bio-instrumentation studies"* as separate study types — the distinction is visually present (asterisked) even if the typical visitor does not parse it.

## Cross-sell / upsell

**From set pages:** no cross-sell to other rituals or to full-size individual products.

**From individual PDPs (the "Suggested Ritual" 4-up):** this is Tatcha's primary cross-sell mechanism. On The Dewy Skin Cream PDP, below the buy box:
- Section heading: `"Suggested Ritual"` in the same serif as product names
- 4 products in a **carousel format** (Previous/Next navigation): The Rice Wash ($40), The Essence ($110), The Dewy Serum ($89), The Dewy Skin Cream ($74)
- Each card: product image + sale price + individual "Add to Bag" button + variant text labels
- Section-level `"Add All to Bag"` button above the carousel

This is the **routine-as-cross-sell** pattern — the PDP cross-sell section functions as a lightweight bundle without a dedicated bundle URL. No combined bundle price is shown; adding all 4 is additive at full prices.

## Subscription default

No subscription or auto-replenish option visible on any Tatcha ritual set or starter ritual page. Tatcha does not surface a subscribe-and-save option on set PDPs or the ritual landing page.

## Voice + visual identity

**Landing page voice:** benefit-led, time-anchored, skin-type specific. *"Reveal Your Glow in Just 2 Weeks"* — the promise is the primary frame. Claims are verb-forward: "proven to," "shown to." The Hadasei-3 ingredient complex is named on landing page product cards but not as a heading — it appears as a bulleted list item. No heritage mythology surfaced on the ritual landing page.

**Individual set page voice:** instructional and aspirational. The "How to Use" sequenced guide uses ritual language: steps are numbered, not bulleted. Application language is sensory: *"Try a complete daily ritual ideal for combo to oily skin."* The word "ritual" appears 3–4 times per set page; "routine" does not appear.

Visual treatment: warm cream/parchment surface throughout. Gold editorial detail (divider lines, micro-icons). No concern-specific accent color per set — all four rituals use the same visual identity. A concern qualifier lives in the text, not in the color. Carousel navigation numbers (*"Go to item 1"*) appear in small sans — unhurried, editorial.

Typography: serif for ritual name (italic on product names; e.g., *"The Dewy Skin Cream"*), sans for concern qualifier (*"Hydrating & Plumping Value Set"*). The two-type system enforces the brand/utility split on every set page.

## What to lift for Clarté

- **Concern-forward set naming on the landing page.** Tatcha's landing page organizes four sets by skin concern (Hydrating / Firming / Clarifying / Calming) with a skin-type qualifier per set (*"ideal for dry skin"*). This is the exact structure Clarté's `/products` page should use to introduce the four protocols — a 4-up grid of protocol tiles, each with a one-line concern qualifier. Apply to the concern-tile row at the top of `/products` (already identified as a target in the page-ux research).

- **One anchored clinical claim per protocol on the landing surface.** Tatcha's landing page gives each ritual one hero-product clinical line above the CTA: *"proven to show a reduction in the look of pores after 2 weeks."* The format is: `"{claim verb} {measurable outcome} {timeframe}"`. Clarté should adopt this pattern on the protocol tile in the homepage 4-up and on the `/products` protocol tier. Once panel data exists, each protocol gets one anchored claim in this format. The key structural rule: the claim lives at the landing/overview level, not buried inside the protocol page — it is the conversion trigger, not the confirmation.

- **The 4-step "How to Use" sequential guide as composition display.** Tatcha's set pages render the included products as a numbered use-order flow (cleanser → prep → treat → moisturize), not just a named list. For Clarté's protocol pages, this maps to: Step 1 (AM cleanser) → Step 2 (active treatment, PM) → Step 3 (moisturizer) → Step 4 (SPF, not yet a Clarté SKU but worth noting). The use-order composition display replaces a flat "included products" list and reinforces the 12-week protocol as a methodology, not a product grouping. Apply to the composition section of `/acne`, `/even-tone`, `/renewal`, `/barrier`.

- **"Suggested Ritual" cross-sell on individual PDPs with "Add All to Bag."** The 4-up cross-sell on Tatcha's PDPs is the most direct model for Clarté's "Complete the Protocol" cross-sell module on individual-product PDPs. Key implementation detail from the Tatcha observation: each product card within the cross-sell has an individual "Add to Bag" plus a section-level "Add All to Bag" — the customer is never forced to commit to the full protocol bundle; they can add one companion item. Map to `app/(site)/products/[sku]/page.tsx`: the `related` section should be named "Complete Your Protocol," query by `protocol_slug`, and carry both an individual add button per product and an "Add Protocol Bundle" CTA above the row.

- **The landing page tagline as a clinical headline, not a marketing headline.** Tatcha's landing page sub-copy leads with *"(up to 32% value)"* parenthetically — the savings angle is quiet, subordinate to the benefit promise. The primary headline is the outcome: *"Reveal Your Glow in Just 2 Weeks."* Clarté's protocol landing page (`/acne`, etc.) should lead with the outcome in italic Fraunces, not the discount or the ingredients. The JetBrains Mono eyebrow carries the clinical anchor; the Fraunces italic carries the outcome promise. Tatcha confirms this hierarchy is correct.

## What to skip

- **No individual product PDP links inside the set composition.** Tatcha deliberately keeps set-product links out of the set page product list — customers who land on the set page stay on the set page. This restraint makes sense for a brand with mature catalog depth (hundreds of SKUs). Clarté, with 8–12 individual SKUs, benefits from the Bader approach (accordion with "View product" links) — it demonstrates protocol-product depth rather than hiding it. Don't follow Tatcha's no-link pattern.

- **"Value Set" as the bundle naming convention.** *"The Starter Ritual: Hydrating & Plumping Value Set"* works for Tatcha because the savings are real ($28–$45 vs individual buy). Clarté's protocols are not discounted vs individual SKUs in the same way — per `feedback_unverified_claims`, no fake strikethroughs. Use "Protocol" as the naming convention, not "Value Set."

- **Heritage ingredient framing (Hadasei-3, Japanese purple rice, Okinawa algae).** The Hadasei-3 proprietary complex is named on set landing pages. For Clarté, the proprietary framework is clinical and protocol-based ("the 12-week method"), not geographic or ingredient-mythology. Do not create a "Clarté complex" name unless the active ingredients justify it on their own clinical terms. Per the anti-heritage-mythology rule.

- **Sitewide discount code on the ritual landing page.** Tatcha's ritual page carries *"Use code: FRIEND26 for 20% off sitewide"* — a promo banner baked into the ritual surface. Clarté has fixed PKR pricing and no discount strategy. Do not surface promo codes on protocol pages — it undermines the clinical positioning.
